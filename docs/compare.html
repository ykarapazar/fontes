Checked 18 November 2026. Written by the author of Fontes, so read it with that in mind. Corrections are welcome: fontesmcp@proton.me or a GitHub issue.

Where Moonlit is likely stronger

  • Semantic search over a curated, uniform index, where Fontes depends on each website's own search.
  • A citation network across its documents.
  • It works in browser apps such as ChatGPT and claude.ai with nothing to install.
  • A company with support behind it.

Where Fontes differs

  • No account and no monthly call limit.
  • It runs on your computer, and your questions go only to the website being searched.
  • It covers more jurisdictions, including many outside Europe.
  • Every text comes from the publisher at the moment you ask, with the link to check it.
  • The code is open, so anyone can see exactly what it sends and where.

And the app on its own?

A model answering from memory can be fluent and wrong: a law since amended, a case number that does not exist. Web search helps, but rarely reaches the court databases themselves. Both tools above exist to put the actual text in front of the model, and in front of you.
