Fontes, Moonlit and Lexifina compared

Moonlit facts checked 18 November 2026; Lexifina facts checked 27 November 2026. Written by the author of Fontes, so read it with that in mind. Corrections are welcome: fontesmcp@proton.me or a GitHub issue.

Four ways to put a legal question to an AI app: Fontes, Moonlit's MCP connector, Lexifina's global-law server, or the app on its own. Fontes depends on Lexifina: it reads Lexifina's record for past versions of laws and for 91 more countries, and is not affiliated with it. Moonlit facts come from moonlit.ai/pricing and its Glama listing, read on the date above. Lexifina facts come from its repository and from Fontes's own use of its server, added on 27 November 2026. We have not tested Moonlit.

Where Moonlit is likely stronger

  • Semantic search over a curated, uniform index, where Fontes depends on each website's own search.
  • A citation network across its documents.
  • It works in browser apps such as ChatGPT and claude.ai with nothing to install.
  • A company with support behind it.

Where Lexifina is stronger

  • The law as it stood on a past date, and what changed it, for about 200 countries. Fontes reads that record for its two past-version tools.
  • A hosted server, so it works in browser apps with nothing to install.
  • Legislation for countries where Fontes has no live adapter.

Compared with Moonlit

  • No account and no monthly call limit.
  • It covers more jurisdictions, including many outside Europe.
  • The code is open, so anyone can see exactly what it sends and where.

Compared with Lexifina

  • Its 125 live jurisdictions are read from each source's own website or API at the moment you ask, with the link to check it. Sources that are not official are flagged in the answer.
  • It also searches case law, which Lexifina's record does not hold.
  • For today's text of Dutch and UK legislation and US Code sections, get_law_on_date fetches the official page and tells you whether Lexifina's record matches it.
  • It respects each publisher's robots.txt and terms for everything it fetches itself.
  • It runs on your computer. Your questions go to the website being searched; for past versions and Lexifina's countries they go to Lexifina's server.

And the app on its own?

A model answering from memory can be fluent and wrong: a law since amended, a case number that does not exist. Web search helps, but rarely reaches the court databases themselves. All the tools above exist to put the actual text in front of the model, and in front of you.

Fontes is a research aid, not legal advice. Read the full decision or provision before you rely on it.
